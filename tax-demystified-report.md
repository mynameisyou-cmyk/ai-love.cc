## 1.2 The 18 Named Strategies

### Trick 1: Carried Interest Loophole
**Who uses it:** Private equity, hedge fund, venture capital partners
**How it works:** Fund managers' profit share (carried interest) is taxed as capital gains (20%) instead of income (37%), even though it's compensation for labor, not investment risk.
**The rule:** Hold the investment for 3+ years → qualify for long-term capital gains rate.
**Cost to public:** $1.5-3 billion/year
**Why most people don't know:** It only affects fund managers, but it sets the precedent that "labor income" can be reclassified as "investment income" if the structure is right.

### Trick 2: Step-Up in Basis at Death
**Who uses it:** Anyone who dies with appreciated assets (real estate, stocks)
**How it works:** When you die, your heirs inherit assets at their CURRENT market value, not your original purchase price. ALL lifetime capital gains are wiped out.
**Example:** You bought stock for $100k. At death it's worth $10M. Your heirs inherit at $10M basis. If they sell immediately, $0 capital gains tax. The $9.9M gain never gets taxed.
**Cost to public:** $40-60 billion/year
**Why most people don't know:** It only matters at death, so people don't plan for it. But it's the engine of the "Buy-Borrow-Die" strategy.

### Trick 3: 1031 Exchange (Like-Kind Exchange)
**Who uses it:** Real estate investors
**How it works:** Sell an investment property, buy another of equal/greater value within 180 days → defer ALL capital gains tax. Can repeat infinitely.
**Example:** Buy for $500k → sell for $5M → buy $5M property → $0 tax on the $4.5M gain. Repeat forever.
**Cost to public:** $5-6 billion/year
**Why most people don't know:** Only applies to real estate, not stocks or other assets.

### Trick 4: Opportunity Zones
**Who uses it:** Real estate developers, investors in designated low-income areas
**How it works:** Invest capital gains into an Opportunity Fund → defer tax on original gain until 2026. Hold for 10 years → pay $0 tax on ALL new appreciation from the Opportunity Zone investment.
**Example:** You have $1M in capital gains. Roll into an OZ fund. After 10 years, the OZ investment grew to $5M. The $4M gain is 100% tax-free.
**Why most people don't know:** The zones are designated by state governors, and the funds are private.

### Trick 5: QSBS (Qualified Small Business Stock, Section 1202)
**Who uses it:** Founders, early employees of startups
**How it works:** If you hold qualified small business stock (C-corp, <$50M assets) for 5+ years, you can exclude $10M or 10x your basis from capital gains tax.
**The stacking trick:** Each family member can claim $10M. A family of 4 = $40M tax-free. Plus, trusts can each claim $10M.
**Example:** You founded a company that sold for $50M. You held the stock 5+ years. $10M is tax-free. If your spouse, children, and a trust each hold shares, that's $40M+ tax-free.
**Why most people don't know:** Only applies to C-corps under $50M assets, and most startup founders don't know to structure as C-corp early.

### Trick 6: QBI Deduction (Section 199A)
**Who uses it:** Pass-through business owners (LLCs, S-corps, sole proprietors)
**How it works:** Deduct 20% (now 23% under OBBBA July 2025) of qualified business income from taxable income.
**Threshold:** Full deduction up to $197,300 (single) / $394,600 (married) in 2025. Above that, limitations phase in.
**Cost to public:** $50-60 billion/year
**Why most people don't know:** It's automatic for most tax software, but the optimization (salary vs. distribution split for S-corps) is not.

### Trick 7: SALT Cap PTE Workaround
**Who uses it:** Business owners in high-tax states (CA, NY, NJ)
**How it works:** The SALT deduction is capped at $10,000. But pass-through entities (PTEs) can pay state taxes at the entity level, which is NOT subject to the $10k cap. The owner gets a federal deduction for the entity-level tax.
**Cost to public:** $10-20 billion/year
**Why most people don't know:** Only available to business owners, not W-2 employees.

### Trick 8: Backdoor Roth & Mega Backdoor Roth
**Who uses it:** High-income earners who exceed Roth IRA income limits
**How it works:**
- **Backdoor Roth:** Contribute to traditional IRA (no income limit), then convert to Roth. Pay tax on any gains, but future growth is tax-free.
- **Mega Backdoor Roth:** If your 401(k) plan allows after-tax contributions, you can contribute up to $46,000 (2025) above the regular $23,500 limit, then convert to Roth. Total Roth capacity: $69,500/year.
**Why most people don't know:** Requires a 401(k) plan that allows after-tax contributions and in-service conversions.

### Trick 9: Buy-Borrow-Die
**Who uses it:** The ultra-wealthy (billionaires)
**How it works:**
1. **Buy:** Acquire appreciating assets (stocks, real estate, businesses)
2. **Borrow:** Take loans against the assets (loans are not taxable income). Live off the loan proceeds.
3. **Die:** Heirs inherit at stepped-up basis. All gains wiped out. The loan is paid from the estate, and the remaining assets pass tax-free (under the $13.99M estate exemption per person).
**Result:** The billionaire pays $0 income tax in their lifetime and $0 capital gains tax at death.
**Why most people don't know:** It only works at scale ($10M+ in assets), and the media never explains it.

### Trick 10: GRAT (Grantor Retained Annuity Trust)
**Who uses it:** Ultra-wealthy families (Walton family used this)
**How it works:** Transfer assets to a trust. You receive annuity payments back for a set term. If the assets appreciate above the IRS Section 7520 rate, the excess passes to heirs gift-tax-free. If they don't appreciate, you get your assets back.
**The "bet your own death" variant:** Set a short term (2 years). If you survive, the appreciation goes to heirs tax-free. If you die, the assets are in your estate anyway.
**Walton family example:** Used GRATs to transfer billions with minimal gift tax.

### Trick 11: Dynasty Trusts
**Who uses it:** Ultra-wealthy families who want to avoid estate tax forever
**How it works:** Transfer assets to an irrevocable trust in a state with no rule against perpetuities (Delaware, Nevada, South Dakota). The trust lasts forever. Each generation benefits from the assets, but the assets never enter anyone's estate.
**GST tax exemption:** The Generation-Skipping Transfer tax exemption ($13.99M per person in 2025) shields the initial transfer. After that, the assets compound tax-free for generations.
**Why most people don't know:** Only matters for families with $14M+ to transfer.

### Trick 12: SLAT (Spousal Lifetime Access Trust)
**Who uses it:** Wealthy couples who want to gift assets but still access them
**How it works:** You gift assets to an irrevocable trust for your spouse's benefit. It removes the assets from your estate (estate tax savings), but your spouse can still receive distributions. When your spouse dies, the assets pass to the next generation.
**The trick:** You keep indirect access through your spouse while removing the assets from your taxable estate.

### Trick 13: Donor-Advised Funds (DAFs)
**Who uses it:** High-income earners who want immediate tax deductions
**How it works:** Contribute cash or appreciated assets to a DAF. Get an immediate tax deduction for the full amount. The DAF holds the funds indefinitely — there's NO required distribution to charity.
**Example:** You have $1M in stock with $100k basis. If you sell, you pay $180k capital gains tax. If you contribute to a DAF, you get a $1M charitable deduction AND pay $0 capital gains tax. The $1M sits in the DAF forever, and you control which charities eventually get it.
**Cost to public:** $230 billion+ parked in DAFs, untaxed

### Trick 14: Transfer Pricing Manipulation
**Who uses it:** Multinational corporations (Apple, Google, Microsoft)
**How it works:** A US parent company sells its intellectual property (patents, trademarks) to a foreign subsidiary in a low-tax country at a low price. The subsidiary then charges the US parent "royalties" for using the IP. The royalties are deductible in the US (reducing US taxable income) and taxed at a low rate abroad.
**Apple Ireland example:** Apple structured two Irish subsidiaries so that one owned the IP and the other licensed it. The licensing entity paid the IP-owning entity royalties, but since both were Irish, the payments were "stateless" — taxed nowhere. Apple's effective rate: 0.005% (yes, five-thousandths of a percent).

### Trick 15: Double Irish Dutch Sandwich
**Who uses it:** US tech giants (Google, Facebook, Microsoft — historically)
**How it works (4 steps):**
1. **Irish Co 1** (incorporated in Ireland, tax-resident in Bermuda) owns the IP. It licenses the IP to Irish Co 2.
2. **Irish Co 2** collects royalties from global operations. It pays royalties to Irish Co 1, but Irish Co 1 is tax-resident in Bermuda (0% tax).
3. **Dutch Co** (Netherlands) sits between Irish Co 1 and Irish Co 2. The royalty payment flows through the Netherlands to avoid Irish withholding tax (EU directive on interest/royalties between EU companies).
4. **Result:** Profits flow from Irish Co 2 → Dutch Co → Irish Co 1 (Bermuda) → taxed at 0%.
**Apple's effective rate:** 0.005%
**Closed:** 2015 (new structures), fully phased out 2020. Legacy structures still exist. Replaced by Single Malt (Malta) and CAIA.

### Trick 16: Corporate Inversions
**Who uses it:** Large US corporations
**How it works:** A US company acquires a smaller foreign company in a low-tax country and re-domiciles the parent there. The US company is now "foreign" and only pays US tax on US-source income.
**Examples:** Medtronic (Ireland), Pfizer (attempted Ireland, blocked), Burger King (Canada), Johnson Controls (Ireland)
**Why most people don't know:** It's legal but politically sensitive. Congress made it harder in 2016, but the loophole isn't fully closed.

### Trick 17: Offshore Tax Havens
**Bermuda:** 0% corporate, 0% income, 0% CGT. $94B+ in profits shifted annually.
**Cayman Islands:** 0% all taxes. $1.5 trillion+ parked. Most US hedge funds are Cayman-domiciled.
**Luxembourg:** ~3% effective rate via SOPARFI holding company regime. $47B shifted.
**Netherlands:** Conduit country. No royalty withholding tax (historically). 100+ tax treaties.
**Switzerland:** Variable cantonal rates. 8.5-15% effective. Known for "fiscal deals" with cantons.

### Trick 18: Bonus Depreciation & R&D Credits
**Bonus depreciation:** 100% immediate deduction for equipment purchases (restored by OBBBA July 2025). Buy a $1M machine → deduct $1M this year instead of depreciating over 7 years.
**R&D credit:** 20% credit for qualifying research expenses (alternative simplified credit: 14%).

# PART 2: UNITED KINGDOM — HMRC

## 2.1 Income Tax (2024-25)

| Band | Rate | Income (2024-25) |
|---|---|---|
| Personal Allowance | 0% | £0 – £12,570 |
| Basic rate | 20% | £12,571 – £50,270 |
| Higher rate | 40% | £50,271 – £125,140 |
| Additional rate | 45% | £125,140+ |

**The 60% Trap:** Between £100,000 and £125,140, every £2 earned above £100k reduces your Personal Allowance by £1. This creates a 60% effective marginal rate.

**Example:** You earn £110,000. Your Personal Allowance is reduced by £5,000 (half the amount above £100k). So you pay 40% on £5,000 that was previously tax-free — effectively 60% on the £10,000 above £100k.

**Fix:** Salary sacrifice, pension contributions, or charitable giving to bring income below £100k.

## 2.2 The 46 Named UK Strategies

### Income Tax Tricks (1-13)

**1. ISA (Individual Savings Account)**
- £20,000/year contribution limit
- All growth and income is 100% tax-free — forever
- Cash ISA, Stocks & Shares ISA, Innovative Finance ISA, Lifetime ISA (£4k/year + 25% government bonus)
- **Everyone should use this.** It's the most powerful tax-free wrapper in the UK.

**2. Salary Sacrifice**
- Instead of receiving salary and buying benefits, you "sacrifice" salary and your employer provides the benefit.
- Saves income tax (20-45%) AND National Insurance (8% employee + 13.8% employer)
- Common uses: pension contributions, electric cars, cycle-to-work, private medical, childcare vouchers
- **Example:** £10,000 pension contribution via salary sacrifice at 40% tax = saves £4,800 in tax + NI

**3. SIPP (Self-Invested Personal Pension)**
- Contribute up to £60,000/year (annual allowance) or 100% of earnings, whichever is lower
- Tax relief at your marginal rate: 20% (basic), 40% (higher), 45% (additional)
- **Example:** A 40% taxpayer contributes £1,000. Net cost: £600. Tax relief adds £400. £1,000 invested for £600 out of pocket.
- Lifetime allowance abolished April 2024 (no more LTA charge!)
- 25% tax-free lump sum at retirement

**4. SSAS (Small Self-Administered Scheme)**
- A pension scheme for business owners/company directors
- Can hold commercial property (your own business premises!)
- Can loan money back to the sponsoring company
- More flexible than SIPP but more complex
- **Who uses it:** Business owners who want to hold property in their pension

**5. Gift Aid**
- Charitable donations get tax relief at your marginal rate
- A 40% taxpayer donates £100. Charity gets £125 (basic rate relief). You claim additional £25 relief on your tax return.
- **Higher/additional rate taxpayers:** Don't forget to claim the additional relief on your tax return!

**6. Dividend vs Salary Optimization**
- Optimal structure for company directors: £12,570 salary (Personal Allowance, £0 tax) + dividends up to higher rate threshold
- Dividend tax rates: 8.75% (basic), 33.75% (higher), 39.35% (additional)
- **The trick:** Pay £12,570 salary (no income tax, no NI if below £12,570). Take the rest as dividends. First £500 dividend allowance is tax-free. Up to £50,270 total = 8.75% (vs 20% income tax + 8% NI).

**7. Marriage Allowance**
- Transfer up to £1,260 of Personal Allowance to spouse/civil partner
- Only if one partner earns below Personal Allowance and the other is a basic rate taxpayer
- Saves up to £252/year

**8. Rent-a-Room Relief**
- Rent out a room in your home: first £7,500/year is tax-free
- Must be furnished residential let, not office

**9. Trading & Property Allowances**
- £1,000 each for miscellaneous trading income and property income
- Below £1,000 = no tax, no reporting
- Above £1,000 = choose: deduct £1,000 or actual expenses

**10. EIS (Enterprise Investment Scheme)**
- 30% income tax relief on up to £1,000,000/year invested in qualifying startups
- CGT deferral: can defer gains on other assets by reinvesting in EIS
- CGT exemption: if you hold EIS shares for 3+ years and sell, the gain is tax-free
- Loss relief: if the company fails, you can offset the loss against income tax
- **Example:** Invest £100,000 in an EIS company. Get £30,000 income tax relief. If the company succeeds and you sell for £500,000 after 3 years, the £400,000 gain is tax-free.

**11. SEIS (Seed Enterprise Investment Scheme)**
- 50% income tax relief on up to £200,000/year
- CGT exemption on gains after 3 years
- Loss relief against income tax
- More generous than EIS but earlier-stage companies (higher risk)

**12. VCT (Venture Capital Trusts)**
- 30% income tax relief on up to £200,000/year
- Tax-free dividends and capital gains
- Listed on stock exchange (more liquid than EIS/SEIS)

**13. Child Benefit HICBC (High Income Child Benefit Charge)**
- Child Benefit starts being clawed back at £60,000 income
- Fully clawed back at £80,000
- **The trick:** If both parents earn £50k each (£100k total), no HICBC. But if one earns £80k and the other £20k, HICBC applies to the £80k earner.

### Capital Gains Tax Tricks (14-20)

**14. BED & ISA**
- Sell investments outside an ISA, buy them back inside an ISA
- Must wait 30 days (bed-and-breakfasting rule) or buy a similar (not identical) fund
- Moves taxable investments into the tax-free ISA wrapper

**15. CGT Annual Exempt Amount**
- £3,000/year (2024-25) — slashed from £6,000 (2023-24), which was slashed from £12,300 (2022-23)
- **The trick:** Use it or lose it. Sell assets to crystallize gains up to £3,000 each year.

**16. Loss Harvesting**
- Sell losing investments to crystallize losses
- Losses offset gains in the same tax year
- Unused losses carry forward indefinitely
- Must report to HMRC within 4 years of the tax year end

**17. BADR (Business Asset Disposal Relief)**
- Formerly Entrepreneurs' Relief
- 10% CGT rate on up to £1,000,000 lifetime gains (2024-25)
- Rising: 14% (2025-26), 18% (2026-27)
- For business sales: sole trader cessation, business asset disposal, company share disposal (if you're an officer/employee with 5%+ stake)

**18. Investors' Relief**
- Same 10% rate as BADR, £1,000,000 lifetime limit
- For external investors (not officers/employees) holding 3+ years
- Shares must be unquoted trading company shares

**19. Spousal Transfers**
- Transfers between spouses are "no gain, no loss"
- You can transfer assets to your spouse to use their CGT annual exemption (£3,000 each = £6,000 total)
- Transfer to the lower-earning spouse to use their basic rate (10% CGT vs 20%)

**20. Rollover & Holdover Relief**
- **Rollover:** Reinvest business asset sale proceeds in new business assets → defer the gain
- **Holdover:** Gift business assets → gain deferred to recipient's base cost

### Corporation Tax Tricks (21-25)

**21. Director's Loan Account**
- Borrow up to £10,000 from your company without tax consequences (no benefit-in-kind)
- Must repay within 9 months after year-end to avoid S455 tax (33.75%)
- **The trick:** Use it for short-term cash flow, not long-term tax avoidance

**22. Full Expensing**
- 100% deduction for qualifying plant and machinery expenditure
- Permanent (announced Autumn 2023)
- **Example:** Buy £50,000 of equipment. Deduct £50,000 from taxable profits this year. At 25% CT, saves £12,500 in tax.

**23. R&D Tax Credits**
- **SME scheme:** 86% enhanced deduction (186% total deduction) + 10% payable cash credit if loss-making
- **RDEC:** 20% above-the-line credit for large companies
- **The trick:** Many companies don't claim because they think R&D = lab coats. In reality, software development, process improvement, and engineering all qualify.

**24. Patent Box**
- 10% corporation tax on profits attributable to patented inventions
- Must hold the patent and have done significant development in-house
- **Example:** Your company has £1M profit from patented products. Normal CT: £250,000 (25%). Patent Box: £100,000 (10%). Saves £150,000.

**25. Corporation Tax Rate Optimization**
- 19% if profits ≤ £50,000
- 25% if profits ≥ £250,000
- Marginal relief between (effective 26.5%)
- **The trick:** For groups, the thresholds are shared across all companies. Splitting profits across companies doesn't bypass this.

### VAT Tricks (26-30)

**26. Flat Rate Scheme**
- For businesses with turnover under £150,000
- Pay a flat rate of VAT based on industry (4% to 16.5%)
- Cannot reclaim VAT on purchases (except capital items over £2,000)
- **Limited cost trader:** If your material costs are low (like consulting), you pay an extra 16.5% flat rate. Defeats the purpose.

**27. Cash Accounting Scheme**
- Account for VAT when customers pay you, not when you invoice
- Helps with cash flow if customers are slow to pay
- For businesses with turnover under £1.5M

**28. Annual Accounting**
- One VAT return per year instead of quarterly
- Make 9 monthly payments on account + one balancing payment
- For businesses with turnover under £1.5M

**29. Margin Scheme**
- For second-hand goods, antiques, collectibles
- Pay VAT on the margin (selling price minus purchase price), not the full selling price
- Common for car dealers, antique dealers

**30. Voluntary Registration**
- Register for VAT even if below £90,000 threshold
- Can reclaim input VAT on purchases
- Makes the business look "bigger" to clients
- **The trick:** If your customers are VAT-registered businesses, voluntary registration is usually a win (they can reclaim your VAT). If your customers are consumers, you're adding 20% to your prices.

### Inheritance Tax Tricks (31-38)

**31. The 7-Year Rule**
- Gifts are tax-free if you survive 7 years after making them
- Taper relief: 3-4 years = 32% IHT, 4-5 = 24%, 5-6 = 16%, 6-7 = 8%
- **The trick:** Start gifting early. The clock starts at the gift date.

**32. Nil Rate Band (NRB)**
- £325,000 per person exempt from IHT
- Unused NRB transfers to surviving spouse (can double to £650,000)

**33. Residence Nil Rate Band (RNRB)**
- Additional £175,000 exemption for passing a home to direct descendants
- Tapers above £2,000,000 estate (loses £1 for every £2 above £2M)
- Combined: £500,000 per person, £1,000,000 for married couples

**34. Gifts Out of Income (UNLIMITED)**
- **The biggest secret in UK IHT:** You can gift UNLIMITED amounts from your surplus income with NO IHT liability and NO 7-year waiting period
- Must be from income, not capital
- Must be part of your normal expenditure
- Must leave you with enough to maintain your standard of living
- **Example:** You earn £200k/year, spend £100k, and regularly gift the remaining £100k to your children. All tax-free, no 7-year rule.

**35. BPR (Business Property Relief)**
- 100% IHT exemption on business assets held 2+ years
- Applies to: unquoted trading company shares, quoted shares where you have 50%+ voting control, business assets used in a trade
- **AIM shares qualify for 100% BPR after 2 years holding**

**36. APR (Agricultural Property Relief)**
- 100% IHT exemption on agricultural property held 2+ years
- Farms, farmland, farmhouses (if character-appropriate)

**37. AIM Shares IHT Exemption**
- Shares listed on the Alternative Investment Market (AIM) qualify for 100% BPR after 2 years
- **The trick:** Invest in AIM shares, hold 2 years, they're 100% IHT-free
- **Risk:** AIM shares are volatile. But for older wealthy individuals, the IHT saving (40%) can outweigh the risk.

**38. Trust Structures**
- **Discretionary trust:** Trustees decide who gets what and when. Income at 45% inside the trust. 10-year periodic charges.
- **Bare trust:** Assets pass absolutely to beneficiary at 18. Simpler, no periodic charges.
- **Interest-in-possession trust:** Beneficiary has right to income for life, capital passes to remaindermen.

### Non-Dom Rules (39-40)

**39. Remittance Basis (2024-25, being abolished)**
- Non-UK-domiciled individuals can choose to be taxed on UK income only, with foreign income untouched unless remitted to the UK
- Remittance Basis Charge: £30,000 (7/9 years resident) or £60,000 (12/14 years)
- Deemed domiciled at 15/20 years for IHT, 15 for income/CGT

**40. New FIG Regime (from April 2025)**
- Abolishes the non-dom concept entirely
- New 4-year tax holiday on foreign income/gains for qualifying new residents (after 10+ years abroad)
- Transitional relief: 50% of foreign income taxable (2025-26), 25% (2026-27)
- Temporary Repatriation Facility: 12% (2025-26) or 15% (2026-27) on remitted foreign income

### Stamp Duty Tricks (41-44)

**41. SDLT Residential Rates**
- 0% to £125k, 2% to £250k, 5% to £925k, 10% to £1.5M, 12% above
- First-time buyer relief: 0% to £300k, 5% to £500k
- Additional property surcharge: +5%
- Non-UK resident surcharge: +2%

**42. LBTT (Scotland)**
- Different rate bands: 0% to £145k, 2% to £250k, 5% to £325k, 10% to £750k, 12% above
- Additional dwelling supplement: +8%

**43. LTT (Wales)**
- 0% to £180k, 3.5% to £250k, 5% to £400k, 7.5% to £750k, 10% to £1.5M, 12% above

**44. SDLT Reliefs**
- No SDLT on: divorce/separation transfers, inheritance, spousal transfers, group relief (between group companies), compulsory purchase replacement

### National Insurance (45-46)

**45. Employee Class 1**
- 8% on £12,570-£50,270 (cut from 12% → 10% → 8% in 2024)
- 2% above £50,270

**46. Employer NIC & Employment Allowance**
- Employer: 13.8% on earnings above £9,100/year per employee
- **Employment Allowance:** £5,000 offset for small businesses
- **The trick:** Salary at £12,570 = no employee NI (below threshold). Employer NI starts at £9,100, but Employment Allowance covers the first £5,000.

## 3.1 The System

## 3.2 Key Tricks

**1. Two-Tier Profits Tax:** First HK$2M of profits at 8.25% (half rate). Above HK$2M at 16.5%.
**Example:** HK$10M profit → first $2M @ 8.25% = $165,000 + $8M @ 16.5% = $1,320,000. Total: $1,485,000. Effective rate: 14.85%.

**2. Salaries Tax Cap:** You pay the LOWER of progressive (2-17%) or flat 15%. For high earners, 15% flat is usually cheaper.

**3. Offshore Income Exemption (FSIE):** Income sourced outside HK is generally tax-free. BUT: post-2023 reform, MNE groups (€750M+ revenue) without economic substance in HK may be taxed on offshore passive income.

**4. No CGT:** If you buy stocks, property, or art and sell for a profit, the gain is NOT taxed. This is one of the biggest tax advantages in the world.

**5. No Estate Duty:** Wealth passes to heirs with no inheritance tax. (Abolished 2006.)

**6. No Dividend Tax:** Dividends from HK companies are tax-free to shareholders.

**7. Patent Box (2025):** Qualifying IP income at 5% — one of the lowest in the world.

**8. Minimum Top-up Tax (MTT):** For MNEs with €750M+ revenue, effective Jan 2025. Brings HK into BEPS Pillar Two compliance.

# PART 4: SINGAPORE — IRAS

## 4.1 The System

| Tax | Rate | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Corporate Tax | 17% | With partial exemption |
| Individual Income Tax | 0-22% | Progressive, top rate 22% above S$320k |
| GST | 9% | Raised Jan 2024 |

| Dividend Tax | **None** | One-tier system (corporate tax is final) |

## 4.2 Key Tricks

**1. Partial Tax Exemption (Corporate):**
- First S$10,000: 75% exempt
- Next S$190,000: 50% exempt
- **Example:** S$200,000 profit → only S$10,000 + S$95,000 = S$105,000 taxable. At 17% = S$17,850. Effective rate: 8.9%.

**2. Start-Up Tax Exemption (first 3 years):**
- First S$100,000: 75% exempt
- Next S$100,000: 50% exempt
- **Example:** S$200,000 profit in year 1 → only S$25,000 + S$50,000 = S$75,000 taxable. At 17% = S$12,750. Effective rate: 6.4%.

**3. VCC (Variable Capital Company):**
- A corporate structure for investment funds
- Sub-funds can be created under one umbrella VCC
- Each sub-fund has separate legal personality
- Tax-transparent for qualifying funds
- Used by hedge funds, private equity, VC

**4. NOR (Not Ordinarily Resident) Scheme:**
- For qualifying expats earning S$160,000+
- Time-apportionment: only the portion of employment income corresponding to days spent in Singapore is taxable
- **Example:** If you spend 60 days in Singapore and 305 days abroad, only 60/365 of your income is taxed in Singapore.

**5. No CGT:** Same as HK — capital gains are not taxed.

**6. ~8% Effective Rate for Foreign MNCs:** Through profit shifting and treaty shopping. $70 billion in profits shifted through Singapore annually.

**7. DTT (Domestic Top-up Tax):** For MNEs under Pillar Two, effective Jan 2025.

## 5.1 Ireland — The BEPS Hub

**The Double Irish Dutch Sandwich (historical):**
1. Irish Co 1 (Bermuda-resident) owns IP
2. Irish Co 2 (Irish-resident) licenses IP, collects global royalties
3. Irish Co 2 pays royalties to Irish Co 1 via Dutch Co (avoids Irish WHT)
4. Irish Co 1 is Bermuda-resident → 0% tax on the royalties
5. Result: profits flow Bermuda → taxed at 0%

**Replacements after 2020:**
- **Single Malt:** Uses a Malta company. Malta refunds 6/7 of corporate tax to shareholders. Effective rate: ~5%.
- **CAIA (Corporate Aircraft Inversion Acquisition):** Apple's tool. Uses an Irish principal company with a Bermuda branch.

## 5.2 Netherlands — The Conduit

- **Historically no royalty withholding tax** (changed 2024 with conditional WHT)
- 100+ tax treaties → perfect conduit country
- CV/BV structures: Dutch BV (limited liability company) holding shares in a foreign CV (commanditaire vennootschap, limited partnership) to avoid tax treaty issues

## 5.3 Luxembourg — SOPARFI

- **SOPARFI (Société de participations financières):** A holding company that benefits from participation exemption
- Dividends and capital gains from qualifying participations (10%+ stake, held 12+ months) are exempt
- Effective rate: ~3%
- $47 billion shifted to Luxembourg annually

## 5.4 Malta — The Refundable Credit

- Headline rate: 35%
- But: 6/7 of the tax is refunded to shareholders when dividends are paid
- Effective rate: ~5%
- Example: $1,000 profit → $350 tax. Shareholder receives dividend + $300 refund. Net tax: $50 (5%).

## 5.5 Cyprus — IP Box + Non-Dom

- **IP Box:** 2.5% effective rate on qualifying IP income (80% exemption + 12.5% on remainder = 2.5%)
- **Non-Dom regime:** 17 years of tax exemption on dividends, interest, and capital gains for non-domiciled residents
- **No IHT:** Estate duty abolished

## 5.6 Global BEPS (Base Erosion and Profit Shifting)

**Scale of the problem:**
- $100-240 billion/year lost globally to BEPS
- Half of all BEPS is by US multinational corporations
- Top 11 BEPS hubs (Ireland, Singapore, Switzerland, Netherlands, Luxembourg, Hong Kong, etc.) receive more shifted profits than the bottom 100 developing countries combined

**OECD Pillar Two — 15% Global Minimum Tax:**
- MNEs with €750M+ consolidated revenue must pay at least 15% effective tax in every jurisdiction
- If a jurisdiction taxes below 15%, the parent's country collects the "top-up tax"
- Effective: 2024-2025
- **Limitations:** Excludes SMEs, funds, government entities. US hasn't fully adopted.

**The Three Main BEPS Techniques:**

1. **IP-based:** Shift IP to low-tax jurisdiction. Charge royalties. (Apple, Google, pharma)
2. **Debt-based (earnings stripping):** Load a subsidiary with debt. Interest payments are deductible. (Private equity)
3. **Transfer pricing:** Overcharge for goods/services between subsidiaries. "Management fees," "service fees."
