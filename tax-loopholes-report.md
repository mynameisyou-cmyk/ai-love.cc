## 1. How to Read This Report

This report explains, in plain language, the legal-but-questionable tricks that multinational corporations and the ultra-wealthy use to shrink their tax bills. For each trick we cover:

- **What it is** — the name and basic idea
- **How it works** — the mechanism, step by step
- **Who benefits** — which corporations, individuals, or sectors
- **What it costs** — estimated revenue lost to public coffers

We are describing *legal* avoidance (using loopholes within the letter of the law), not illegal evasion (lying, hiding, fraud). The scandal is that so much of this is perfectly legal.

> **Key distinction:** Tax *evasion* = illegal. Tax *avoidance* = legal, exploiting loopholes. Tax *planning* = the polite word the industry uses for avoidance. This report is about avoidance.

Before the country-by-country breakdown, here is the scale of the problem.

## 3. United Kingdom — UK

### 3.1 Diverted Profits Tax ("the Google Tax")

- **What:** A 25% (raised to 31% in 2023) tax on profits artificially shifted out of the UK, introduced in 2015 to combat the "Double Irish" style schemes.
- **How it works:** Companies move profits to lower-tax jurisdictions using internal pricing arrangements. The DPT charges a higher rate than the main corporation tax (currently 25%) on those shifted profits, forcing companies to justify their arrangements.
- **Who benefits:** Tech giants (Google, Apple, Facebook/Meta) historically avoided UK tax by routing sales through Ireland. The DPT aimed to curb this but raised relatively little in early years (e.g., ~£180m in 2016-17) because companies restructured to stay just inside the rules.
- **Cost to taxpayers:** The original UK corporation tax losses from profit-shifting were estimated at £5–10bn/year before reforms. DPT itself raised only a fraction — the real fix came later via OECD Pillar Two (see §10).

### 3.2 Capital Gains — The "Buy, Borrow, Die" Strategy

- **What:** The ultra-wealthy rarely *sell* appreciating assets, so they rarely pay capital gains tax (CGT). Instead they **borrow against their wealth**.
- **How it works:**
  1. Founder holds shares worth £100m. If they sold, they'd pay CGT (~20–24% on gains above allowances).
  2. Instead, they take a secured loan from a bank using shares as collateral — say £20m to live on.
  3. Loans are not income, so **no tax is owed**. The £20m is tax-free cash.
  4. They pay only interest (much cheaper than CGT).
  5. On death, assets pass to heirs. UK rules allow **"uplift"** — the asset is rebased to its market value at death, so heirs can sell immediately with no CGT on the lifetime of gain. The lifetime gain is *wiped out for CGT purposes*.
  6. Inheritance tax (IHT) may apply at 40% — but see §3.3 for how that's dodged.
- **Who benefits:** Billionaire founders, business owners, landlords — anyone with large unrealised gains.
- **Cost:** UK CGT and IHT losses are estimated in the **low billions** annually. The "uplift" alone is worth billions to wealthy estates.
- **Why it matters:** A teacher pays 20–45% income tax on every paycheck. A billionaire can live tax-free on borrowed money.

### 3.3 Inheritance Tax Avoidance — Trusts, AIM & BPR

- **What:** IHT nominally taxes estates at 40% above £325,000 (£500,000 for residence). The effective rate the wealthy pay is far lower.
- **How it works:**
  - **Business Property Relief (BPR) & Agricultural Property Relief (APR):** Shares in AIM-listed companies (often called "IHT-free portfolios") qualify for 100% relief after 2 years. Wealthy investors build AIM portfolios specifically to dodge IHT — this is openly marketed by wealth managers as "the AIM inheritance tax shelter."
  - **Trusts:** Assets put into discretionary trusts leave the estate. While there's a 20% "entry charge" above the nil band and periodic charges, the trust structure defers/ reduces tax and keeps assets outside the taxable estate. Family Investment Companies (FICs) also do this and are hugely popular with the wealthy — Richard Branson's and the Bamford (JCB) family's structures are famous examples.
  - **Lifetime gifts:** Anything given away and survived for 7 years falls out of the estate. "Potentially Exempt Transfers" let the wealthy drain their estate over time.
  - **Agricultural relief:** Landowning families pass farms tax-free, even when worth tens of millions.
- **Who benefits:** Landowners, business owners, wealthy families — NOT ordinary families whose main asset is a house worth just over the threshold.
- **Cost:** BPR alone costs the Treasury an estimated **£1.5–2bn/year** and rising fast as AIM shelters boom. APR adds several hundred million more.
- **Result:** Effective IHT rate for large estates is ~12–20%, not 40%. The richest 5% of estates pay a *lower* effective rate than the next 40%.

### 3.4 Non-Dom Status (Being Reformed)

- **What:** "Non-domiciled" residents — people living in the UK but claiming their permanent home ("domicile") is abroad — historically paid UK tax only on UK income/gains. Foreign income and gains were untaxed, even while living in London, as long as they didn't bring it ("remit") to the UK.
- **How it works:** After 15 years, non-doms got taxed on arising basis. Before that, foreign income was tax-free unless remitted. The £30,000 / £60,000 "Remittance Basis Charge" applied after 7/12 years but was trivial for billionaires.
- **Who benefits:** Super-rich foreigners living in the UK (oligarchs, sheikhs, finance executives, inherited-wealth families).
- **Cost:** Estimated **£3–4bn/year** lost.
- **Reform (2024/25):** Labour government abolished the non-dom regime from April 2025, replacing it with a 4-year "FIG" (Foreign Income and Gains) regime for genuine new arrivals. A major victory — though the new 4-year window still offers a temporary haven.

### 3.5 Stamp Duty Land Tax (SDLT) — Corporate Envelopes

- **What:** Residential property over £500k held via a company used to dodge the 15% higher-rate SDLT and the Annual Tax on Enveloped Dwellings (ATED).
- **How it works:** Buy a £20m mansion through a BVI or Channel Islands company. Sell the *company* (not the house) — no SDLT on the property transfer, just a share transfer offshore. New 2022 rules (the "15% SDLT on corporate envelopes") and ATED cracked down on residential, but commercial property "sale and leaseback" and SDLT sub-sale reliefs remain heavily used.
- **Cost:** Hundreds of millions in SDLT avoided before reforms; still used for commercial property.

### 3.6 The "City" & Private Equity — Carried Interest

- **What:** Private equity executives' profit-share ("carried interest" — typically 20% of fund profits) is taxed as **capital gains**, not income.
- **How it works:** Fund managers get "2 and 20" — 2% management fee (taxed as income, 45%) + 20% of profits above a hurdle (taxed as CGT, 28% with Business Asset Disposal Relief now reducing to 14% then 18% in 2025/26).
- **Who benefits:** Private equity and venture capital partners. Average large-fund partner earns £millions/year; the gap between 45% income tax and ~14% CGT is enormous.
- **Cost:** Estimated **£600m–£2bn/year** depending on assumptions. Labour pledged to raise carried interest to income tax rates — implementation pending.

### 3.7 Film, Theatre & "Creative" Reliefs

- **What:** EIS/SEIS, Film Tax Relief, and the (now-closed) Enterprise Zone schemes let wealthy individuals offset income tax by investing in films or startups.
- **How it works:** Invest £50k in an SEIS-qualifying startup, get 50% income tax relief (£25k back) + CGT exemption + loss relief if it fails. Risk is largely underwritten by the Treasury.
- **Who benefits:** High-earning individuals (bankers, doctors, consultants) topping out their tax bill; startup founders; film producers.
- **Cost:** EIS/SEIS cost ~£500–700m/year. Intentional policy — but heavily skewed to richer taxpayers (you need spare cash to invest).

### 3.8 VAT & the Flat Rate / Missing Trader Schemes

- **What:** "Missing Trader Intra-Community" (MTIC) VAT fraud — "carousel fraud" — costs billions.
- **How it works:** Trader A in UK sells goods to Trader B in another EU state (zero-rated VAT cross-border). B sells back to A's shell company in UK charging VAT but disappears without paying HMRC. Repeat in a "carousel."
- **Cost:** HMRC estimates £400m–£1bn+/year despite enforcement. Largely criminal but exploits the VAT design itself.

## 4. United States — US

### 4.1 The "Buy, Borrow, Die" Strategy (the US version)

- **What:** The single biggest reason American billionaires pay lower effective tax rates than their secretaries. Famously documented by ProPublica in 2021 ("The Secret IRS Files").
- **How it works:**
  1. Asset appreciates massively (Berkshire Hathaway stock, Amazon shares). **No tax on appreciation until sold.**
  2. Owner borrows against the asset to fund lifestyle — loans are **not income**, no tax.
  3. Interest is often **deductible** if secured against investments, further reducing other income.
  4. On death, **"step-up in basis"** wipes out all unrealised gains. Heirs inherit at the *current market value* and can sell tax-free. The entire lifetime of gain is never taxed.
- **Who benefits:** Bezos, Musk, Buffett, Ellison — every US billionaire dynasty. ProPublica found Jeff Bezos paid **zero** federal income tax in 2007 and 2011, and reported income so low in some years he claimed the child tax credit.
- **Cost:** Wyden/Senate estimates suggest taxing unrealised gains for the ultra-rich would raise **~$500bn over a decade**. The step-up in basis alone costs the Treasury **~$40bn/year**.

### 4.2 Carried Interest — The Hedge/PE Loophole

- **What:** Fund managers' profit-share taxed as long-term capital gains (max 20% + 3.8% NIIT) instead of ordinary income (up to 37%).
- **How it works:** "Carried interest" is technically an equity stake in the fund, so profits (held 3+ years under current rules) qualify for long-term CGT. A PE partner earning $20m/year pays ~23.8% vs a doctor's 37%+.
- **Who benefits:** Private equity, hedge fund, venture capital partners.
- **Cost:** Closing it would raise **~$15bn over 10 years** (CBO). Every Democratic primary candidate has pledged to close it; it survives because the industry lobbies hard. Trump's 2017 "reform" only lengthened the holding period from 1 to 3 years.

### 4.3 Real Estate "Like-Kind" (1031) Exchanges

- **What:** Under §1031, if you swap one investment property for another, you defer all capital gains tax — forever, if you keep swapping.
- **How it works:** Sell a building for $50m gain → immediately buy another of equal value → pay $0 CGT now. Repeat until death, when step-up wipes the slate clean.
- **Who benefits:** Real estate moguls (Trump famously used this), landlords, REIT investors.
- **Cost:** ~$5–6bn/year in deferred tax. The 2017 TCJA narrowed 1031 to real estate only (previously applied to art, equipment) but left the real estate carve-out intact.

### 4.4 Accelerated Depreciation & Bonus Depreciation

- **What:** Companies write off the cost of assets (buildings, equipment, jets) far faster than they actually wear out, cutting current-year tax.
- **How it works:** TCJA allows 100% "bonus depreciation" (phasing down 2023–27) — buy a $50m private jet, deduct $50m immediately against profits. A factory's cost is recovered in 5–10 years even though it lasts 40.
- **Who benefits:** Capital-intensive businesses, manufacturers, anyone buying private jets (literally — Section 179 lets small businesses expense jets).
- **Cost:** Bonus depreciation alone: ~$50–80bn/year in foregone revenue.

### 4.5 Offshore Profit Shifting (Pre-Pillar Two)

- **What:** US multinationals book profits in Ireland, Bermuda, Luxembourg, the Netherlands, Caribbean — places with near-zero corporate tax.
- **How it works:** The IP-royalty trick (see §9.3 Transfer Pricing). Apple booked the bulk of its non-US profits in Ireland at an effective rate of ~1% for years. The 2017 TCJA's GILTI and BEAT provisions aimed to curb this but were riddled with exceptions.
- **Who benefits:** Big Tech, pharma (Pfizer, Johnson & Johnson), consumer goods.
- **Cost:** ITEP estimates US Fortune 500 companies avoid **~$60–90bn/year** federal corporate tax via profit shifting. Effective US corporate tax rate for large profitable companies averages ~8–10%, not the headline 21%.

### 4.6 S-Corporation "Pass-Through" Loophole

- **What:** TCJA gave "pass-through" entities (S-corps, LLCs, partnerships) a 20% deduction on qualified business income (QBI, §199A).
- **How it works:** A consultant earning $2m through an S-corp pays themselves a "reasonable salary" of $200k (paying payroll tax on that) and takes the rest as "distributions" — no payroll tax, and gets the 20% QBI deduction. Trump's own businesses used this.
- **Who benefits:** High-earning professionals, real estate partnerships, hedge funds structured as pass-throughs.
- **Cost:** §199A costs ~$50–60bn/year; the S-corp payroll dodge costs Social Security/Medicare **several billion more**.

### 4.7 Trust & Dynasty Trusts

- **What:** "Generation-skipping" and dynasty trusts in states like **South Dakota, Nevada, Delaware** (the US's own onshore tax havens) let families lock up wealth for centuries, growing tax-free, with no federal gift/estate tax.
- **How it works:** South Dakota abolished the rule against perpetuities. Assets in a SD dynasty trust never re-enter the taxable estate. Trust income is tax-free if invested in tax-exempt bonds or if structured to produce no taxable distributions. Wealth planners openly market "perpetual trusts."
- **Who benefits:** Old-money dynasties (Mars, Walton, DuPont, Bessemer Trust) and new money alike.
- **Cost:** US estate/gift tax collects only ~$20–30bn/year against a multi-trillion-dollar transfer of wealth. Effectively, the *de jure* 40% estate tax applies to <0.1% of deaths and even then, at a low effective rate.

### 4.8 Opportunity Zones

- **What:** TCJA lets investors defer and reduce capital gains by reinvesting in "Opportunity Zone" funds.
- **How it works:** Sell any asset with a gain → put the gain in an OZ fund → defer tax to 2026, pay only 90% of the gain, and if held 10 years, **pay zero tax on the OZ investment's own gain**.
- **Who benefits:** Real estate developers, wealthy investors. Many OZ projects were luxury condos, hotels, and student housing in already-gentrifying areas — not the distressed communities the law promised.
- **Cost:** ~$1–10bn/year depending on take-up. ECONorthwest found OZ money flowed disproportionately to already-developing tracts.

### 4.9 Stock Options & "Borrow Against the Company"

- **What:** Tech founders take $1 salary and receive stock options that vest over years; they control when they "exercise" and trigger tax.
- **How it works:** Elon Musk famously paid zero income tax in 2018. He exercises options in low-income years and borrows against shares in others.

## 5. European Union — EU

### 5.1 The "Double Irish with a Dutch Sandwich"

- **What:** The most infamous tax structure in history, used by Google, Apple, Facebook, Microsoft, Oracle, Adobe, and dozens more.
- **How it works:**
  1. **Irish Co. A** (incorporated in Ireland, tax-resident in Bermuda) owns the IP. It licenses the IP to...
  2. **Irish Co. B** (incorporated *and* tax-resident in Ireland) which sells to non-US customers and collects the revenue.
  3. Co. B pays huge royalties to Co. A for the IP — these royalties are tax-deductible in Ireland.
  4. **The "Dutch Sandwich":** Because Irish withholding tax would apply to direct Ireland→Bermuda royalties, the money first goes to a **Dutch BV** (no withholding tax on EU royalties) then on to Co. A in Bermuda. The BV is just a postbox.
  5. Result: Billions of European revenue end up in Bermuda taxed at 0%.
- **Who benefits:** Big Tech. Apple famously paid an effective Irish rate of **0.005%** in 2014 (per the EU Commission state-aid case).
- **Cost:** The structure routed tens of billions a year out of Europe. Ireland finally closed the "Double Irish" in 2015 — but grandfathered existing arrangements **until 2020**. Apple's arrangement alone shielded ~$100bn+ of profits.

### 5.2 The Dutch "CV/BV" and Other Holding-Company Sandwiches

- **What:** The Netherlands is the world's second-largest conduit for offshore capital after the US, with thousands of letterbox companies used to channel royalties and dividends tax-free.
- **How it works:** Dutch holding-company law lets profits flow through with no withholding tax on royalties and dividends to non-EU recipients. "CV/BV" structures combine a Dutch holding BV with a special "commanditaire vennootschap" partnership to make income vanish from both Dutch *and* the recipient country's tax base.
- **Cost:** Tax Justice Network estimates Dutch conduit structures enable tens of billions/year in tax avoidance. Ireland, Netherlands, Luxembourg, and Belgium are the "Axis of Avoidance" within the EU.

### 5.3 Luxembourg's "Tax Rulings" (LuxLeaks)

- **What:** Leaked in 2014 by Antoine Deltour, ~28,000 pages revealed that Luxembourg gave secret advance tax rulings ("comfort letters") to 340+ companies — Disney, IKEA, Pepsi, FedEx, Burberry, Koch Industries — letting them route profits through Luxembourg at near-zero effective rates.
- **How it works:** A Luxembourg holding company borrows from a sister company in a high-tax country (creating deductible interest there), then "lends" to another affiliate. The Luxembourg entity pays almost no tax because of generous notional interest deductions and amortisation.
- **Who benefits:** Hundreds of multinationals.
- **Cost:** Estimates range into the tens of billions/year across the EU.

### 5.4 Malta — The EU's Internal Tax Haven

- **What:** EU member Malta offers an effective corporate tax rate of **5%** via its refund system.
- **How it works:** Nominal corporate tax is 35%, but when profits are distributed, shareholders get back 6/7 of the tax → effective 5%. Combined with imputation and gaming of residency rules, many online gaming, fintech, and iGaming companies book profits in Malta.
- **Who benefits:** Online gambling companies (Betsson, Kindred/Unibet), financial firms, wealthy individuals. "Malta" was central to the 2019 #GamingIndustry scandal.
- **Cost:** Estimated €3–5bn/year lost to other EU states; Malta's own €1.4bn national budget is dwarfed by the profits routed there.

### 5.5 VAT Carousel ("MTIC") Fraud

- **What:** Cross-border VAT fraud exploiting the EU's zero-rated cross-border B2B sales.
- **How it works:** Trader in Country A sells zero-rated to B in Country B. B charges VAT to consumer, then vanishes without remitting. "Carousel" versions cycle the same goods (often electronics, CO2 permits) repeatedly.
- **Cost:** EU Commission estimates €50–60bn/year lost.

### 5.6 Harmful Tax Competition — The Race to the Bottom

- **What:** Member states undercut each other on corporate rates to attract HQ relocations — effectively bribing multinationals at the expense of EU citizens.
- **Examples:** Ireland's 12.5% (now 15% for big groups under Pillar Two), Hungary's 9%, Bulgaria's 10%, Cyprus's 12.5%.

## 6. Hong Kong

### 6.1 Territorial Tax System

- **What:** Hong Kong taxes only income *sourced in HK*. Foreign-sourced income — dividends, royalties, interest, capital gains from abroad — is **completely tax-free**.
- **How it works:** A HK holding company receives dividends from a Mainland China or overseas subsidiary: zero HK tax. Sell overseas assets: zero HK CGT. This makes HK an ideal regional holding hub and the gateway for money flows into/out of China.
- **Who benefits:** Multinationals (especially Mainland-Chinese firms using HK as offshore HQ), family offices, wealthy individuals.
- **Cost (global loss):** TJN lists HK as the **world's biggest enabler of financial secrecy** — its 2022 ranking put HK #1 on the Financial Secrecy Index. Estimated global tax loss from HK conduit structures runs into the tens of billions/year.

### 6.2 Low Corporate Tax + Offshore Fund Exemption

- **What:** HK profits tax is **16.5%** (8.25% for first HK$2m). "Offshore funds" exempt from profits tax entirely. No capital gains tax. No VAT. No dividend withholding.
- **How it works:** Hedge funds and private equity structure via Cayman funds managed from HK. The HK managers take "carried interest" — and a discretionary **carried-interest tax concession** caps the effective rate at roughly **8.25–15%**, far below income tax.
- **Who benefits:** Hedge funds, PE, family offices, the ~10,000+ family offices that HK actively courts.

### 6.3 The Big Reform (2025) — FSIE

- **What:** Bowing to international pressure (and EU "grey list" threats), HK introduced a **Foreign-Sourced Income Exemption (FSIE)** regime from 2023/25.
- **How it works:** Foreign-sourced *passive* income (interest, dividends, IP royalties) is now taxed at 16.5% *unless* certain substance/economic substance requirements are met (a HK office, employees, expenditure). Pure shell structures no longer escape.
- **Impact:** HK is being slowly pulled into the global minimum-tax net, but it remains a low-tax jurisdiction and a secrecy hub. Capital is increasingly relocating to Singapore (see §7) in the "HK → SG" wealth migration that began after 2019.

## 7. Singapore

### 7.1 Territorial-ish Low Tax + The "Fund Incentive" Regimes

- **What:** Singapore corporate tax is **17%** flat, but a web of "incentives" (Tax Exemption for Investment Managers, 13O/13U/13D funds) can reduce fund income tax to near zero.
- **How it works:**
  - A family office sets up a fund vehicle under section **13O/13U** of the Income Tax Act → designated investments are **tax-exempt**.
  - Requires a minimum AUM (now $20m for 13O, $50m for 13U) and local spend/job creation — but easily met by wealthy families.
  - "Global Trader Programme" gives concessionary **5% or 10%** rates to commodity traders.
- **Who benefits:** ~1,400+ single-family offices now registered in Singapore (up from a few hundred in 2018), hedge funds, commodity traders, ultra-wealthy Asians and increasingly Westerners seeking a bolt-hole.
- **Cost:** Singapore is now ranked **#2 on the Financial Secrecy Index** behind HK (per TJN 2022). The "HK→SG" wealth migration post-2019 saw hundreds of billions in assets relocate. Tax Justice Network estimates Singapore enables tens of billions/year in global tax loss.

### 7.2 No Capital Gains Tax + Generous Holding-Company Regime

- **What:** No CGT. Dividends received from foreign subsidiaries are generally exempt. No estate duty (abolished 2008). No tax on capital gains for individuals.
- **How it works:** A wealthy individual holds global assets via a Singapore holding company, sells them, pays nothing. Used as a "whitewash" jurisdiction: a place that is *technically* transparent and compliant while still offering zero tax on the right income types.

### 7.3 Variable Capital Companies (VCCs)

- **What:** Introduced 2020, the VCC is an umbrella fund structure designed to attract more fund capital by offering privacy and tax efficiency.
- **How it works:** Sub-funds within a VCC are legally segregated, can be open- or closed-ended, and enjoy the same tax incentives as 13O/13U funds. Designed explicitly to compete with Cayman and Dublin as a fund domicile.

### 8.3 The UK's Role — "The World's Biggest Tax Haven Network"

- The Tax Justice Network famously called **the UK, its Crown Dependencies, and Overseas Territories** "the world's biggest tax haven network" — collectively supplying **over a third of global financial secrecy**.
- UK-controlled jurisdictions include: BVI, Cayman, Bermuda, Turks & Caicos, Anguilla, Montserrat, Gibraltar, Jersey, Guernsey, Isle of Man, Akrotiri & Dhekeli.
- The UK Parliament has constitutional authority to legislate for the Crown Dependencies and most Overseas Territories. Public registers of beneficial ownership were repeatedly demanded (e.g., the Sanctions and Anti-Money Laundering Act 2018) and repeatedly resisted by local governments — until 2023, when Cayman and BVI began implementing public registers under EU pressure.

### 8.4 How Offshore Shells Work — A Plain Example

**Setup:** You're a wealthy Brazilian family worth $500m.

1. **Set up a BVI Business Company** — anonymous, costs ~$1,000/year, no tax, no public ownership records (until very recently).
2. **Open a Swiss or Singapore bank account** in the BVI company's name. The bank knows you ("KYC") but the world doesn't.
3. **Buy a yacht, a London flat, a Manhattan penthouse** — all titled to the BVI company. To sell, you sell the *company*, not the asset. No real estate transaction, no stamp duty, no public record.
4. **Layer in a trust** in the Cook Islands or Liechtenstein to "own" the BVI company — adds asset-protection (creditors can't easily pierce it) and another layer of secrecy.
5. **Add a foundation** in Panama or Lichtenstein to be the trust's settlor, and a nominee director in Cyprus to be the company's "director."

Result: an impenetrable web of paper entities that owns real assets, pays no tax anywhere, and is invisible to your home country's tax authority.

**The leak:** Only leaks — **Panama Papers (2016), Paradise Papers (2017), Pandora Papers (2021), FinCEN Files (2020), Suisse Secrets (2022)** — have ever given the public a glimpse. The Pandora Papers exposed 35 current/former world leaders, 330+ politicians, and 100+ billionaires using offshore secrecy. The FinCEN Files showed $2 trillion+ in suspicious transactions banks reported and did nothing about.

### 8.5 Trust Structures — The Engine of Secrecy

- **What:** A trust separates legal ownership (the trustee) from beneficial enjoyment (the beneficiary) — so assets don't "belong" to the wealthy person whose wealth it is.
- **How it works:**
  - Wealthy **Settlor** transfers assets to a **Trustee** (a corporate trustee in Jersey or Bermuda) to hold for the **Beneficiaries** (the family).
  - The settlor has *no legal ownership* → no asset appears on their estate, in their divorce settlement, or in their tax return.
  - A **Letter of Wishes** lets the settlor direct the trustee without "owning."
  - Combined with **Purpose Trusts** (no identifiable beneficiaries) and **STAR trustss** in Cayman, you can hold assets for a family's benefit with no individual "owning" anything.
- **Why it matters:** Trusts are a uniquely Anglo-Saxon legal invention. The UK and its former colonies (Cayman, BVI, Jersey) are the global trust capital. Civil-law countries don't have them — and have spent decades demanding they be made transparent.

## 9. Cross-Cutting Schemes Explained in Plain Language

### 9.1 Transfer Pricing — "Selling to Yourself"

- **The trick:** A multinational has subsidiaries in many countries. It sets the *internal* prices at which those subsidiaries trade with each other. By manipulating these prices, it books profits wherever taxes are lowest.
- **Plain example:**
  - A US sneaker company makes shoes in Vietnam for $20. Sells them worldwide for $100.
  - It sets up a subsidiary in Bermuda (0% tax) that "owns the brand" — the swoosh.
  - Every subsidiary that sells shoes "pays royalties" to the Bermuda company for the brand. Suddenly the $80 profit per shoe isn't in the US or Germany (taxed at 25%+) — it's in Bermuda (0%).
- **The rule that should stop it:** OECD says internal prices must be "arm's length" — what independent parties would charge. But brands, software, and IP are hard to value, so companies game it.
- **Cost:** OECD estimates 4% of global GDP — roughly **$427bn/year** — is lost to profit shifting of which transfer pricing manipulation is the biggest chunk.
- **Fix attempts:** OECD's BEPS project (2013–), country-by-country reporting, Pillar One (reallocation of taxing rights) and Pillar Two (15% global minimum, see §10).

### 9.2 The "Double Irish with a Dutch Sandwich" (recap)

See §5.1. The canonical example. Closed in 2020 but its DNA lives on in successor structures (e.g., "Single Irish," "Green Jersey," and IP-routing through Singapore).

### 9.3 IP & Royalty Routing — "The Brand Pays You"

- **The trick:** Put your company's brand, patent, or software IP in a low-tax country. Charge your high-tax-country subsidiaries "royalties" for using it. Profit shifts to the low-tax country.
- **Examples:** Apple's Irish IP-residence trick, Starbucks paying royalties to a Dutch subsidiary for "coffee-roasting know-how," Nike paying a Bermuda subsidiary to license the "Nike" name itself.

### 9.4 Thin Capitalisation / Interest Deduction

- **The trick:** Fund your foreign subsidiary with *debt* rather than equity. The subsidiary pays "interest" back to the parent — interest is deductible in the high-tax country, taxed (lightly or not at all) in the parent's country. Money moves out tax-free.
- **The lever:** The 2017 EU Anti-Tax-Avoidance Directive (ATAD) capped interest deductions at 30% of EBITDA, but the rule has many exceptions and "group ratio" waivers.

### 9.5 Hybrid Mismatches

- **The trick:** Use instruments that one country treats as debt (deductible) and another as equity (non-taxed), or entities one country treats as a company and another as transparent. Result: deduction in Country A, no inclusion in Country B.
- **Example:** ATAD II specifically targeted these, but they persist.

### 9.6 Check-the-Box & "Disregarded Entities" (US)

- **The trick:** A US multinational's foreign subsidiary is "checked" to be disregarded for US tax, so a loan between two disregarded entities is treated as a loan from the parent *to itself*. Pre-TCJA this allowed "hopscotch" loans that bypassed US tax on repatriations.

### 9.7 Patent Boxes / IP Regimes

- **The trick:** Many countries (UK, Ireland, Luxembourg, Belgium, Netherlands, Hungary, Cyprus, Spain, Portugal, China, Switzerland) offer a reduced tax rate (often 5–10%) on income derived from patents and software developed locally.
- **The abuse:** Companies can shift IP *into* the patent box from abroad (the "modified nexus" approach was supposed to require local R&D, but is gamed).

### 9.8 Inversion & Corporate Re-Domicile

- **The trick:** A US company "inverts" — merges with a smaller Irish/Bermudan company and re-headquarters abroad — to escape US worldwide taxation. Burger King → Tim Hortons (Canada, 2014), Medtronic → Covidien (Ireland, 2015). The 2017 TCJA + Treasury rules largely killed new inversions, but the playbook migrated to "headquarters in Ireland" for new companies.

### 9.9 Crypto & Decentralised Avoidance (emerging)

- **The trick:** Wealth moves to stablecoins, DeFi, and offshore crypto exchanges with no KYC/AML reporting. Pseudonymous wallets, mixers (Tornado Cash), and crypto-friendly jurisdictions (UAE, El Salvador, Portugal's former crypto-tax holiday, Cayman) enable a new generation of avoidance.

### 9.10 The "Family Investment Company" (FIC)

- **What:** A private UK limited company whose shareholders are family members (often as different share classes). The FIC holds investments; profits are taxed at corporation tax (25%), and dividends can be drip-fed to family members using their personal allowances — instead of all income landing on the high-earner at 45%.
- **Famous users:** Branson (Virgin), the Bamford family (JCB), Lord Ashcroft.
- **Cost:** HMRC has begun litigating FICs under the "settlements" legislation; outcomes pending.

## 10. Progressive Reforms & Grassroots Movements

### 10.1 OECD Pillar Two — The 15% Global Minimum Tax

- **What:** ~140 countries agreed (2021) that large multinationals (>$750m revenue) must pay **at least 15% effective tax** in every jurisdiction they operate in. If a subsidiary's profits are taxed below 15% in a haven, the parent's home country tops it up.
- **Status:** In force from 2024 in the EU, UK, Japan, South Korea, Canada, Australia and others. The US has *not* adopted it (its GILTI regime is loosely aligned but not compliant), creating friction.
- **Limitations:** 15% is too low (campaigners wanted 21%); excludes investment funds and real estate; "safe harbours" exempt many MNEs temporarily; doesn't help developing countries much because the "top-up" tax is collected at HQ, not the source country.
- **Estimated revenue:** OECD projects **$155–192bn/year** globally once fully implemented.

### 10.2 Pillar One — Reallocating Taxing Rights

- **What:** Some taxing rights on the world's 100 biggest, most profitable MNEs would be reallocated to the *market* countries (where the customers are) — not just the HQ country. Designed to address "digital" companies that sell into a country with no physical presence.
- **Status:** Stalled. The US under Trump 2.0 has walked away; the compromise "Amount B" was watered down to optional. Effectively on life support.

### 10.3 Public Country-by-Country Reporting

- **What:** MNEs must publish revenue, profit, tax paid, and employees *per country*. EU adopted this (2021, in force 2024). UK implemented 2024. US SEC proposed it; Trump-era rollback killed it; status unclear.
- **Why it matters:** Reveals where profits are booked vs. where business actually happens — exposing the gap.

### 10.4 Beneficial Ownership Registers

- **What:** Public databases showing the real human behind every shell company and trust.
- **Status:** EU requires public BO registers for companies (5th AML Directive); trusts' registers are restricted to competent authorities. UK introduced a register of overseas owners of UK property (2022) and is implementing a trust register. BVI and Cayman finally launched public registers in 2023–24 after sustained pressure. The US still has no federal beneficial ownership *public* register (the Corporate Transparency Act gives FinCEN access only, and is under legal/political attack).

### 10.5 Wealth Taxes — A Live Debate

- **What:** An annual tax on net worth (not income). Spain has introduced a temporary solidarity wealth tax; Argentina's "Impuesto al Patrimonio" exists; Norway, Spain, Switzerland have net-wealth taxes.
- **Proposals:**
  - **US:** Senator Elizabeth Warren's "Ultra-Millionaire Tax" — 2% on wealth >$50m, 3% >$1bn. Scored at ~$3 trillion/decade.
  - **EU:** A coordinated EU wealth tax (Thomas Piketty's "Euro 1%" for the Eurozone).
  - **UK:** A "1% wealth tax on £1m+ assets" — LSE's Wealth Tax Commission estimated it could raise £260bn over 5 years.
- **The blockers:** Valuation challenges, capital flight, political opposition, lack of international coordination.

### 10.6 Taxing Unrealised Gains ("Billionaire Income Tax")

- **What:** Senator Ron Wyden's "Billionaires Income Tax" would tax unrealised capital gains annually for those with >$1bn wealth or >$100m income for 3 consecutive years.
- **Cost:** ~$360–500bn over a decade.
- **Status:** Not enacted; opposed by moderate Democrats and the entire Republican party.

### 10.7 Taxing Carried Interest as Income

- **What:** Tax carried interest at ordinary income rates, not CGT.
- **Where:** US (Wyden/Obama/Biden proposals); UK (Labour's 2024 manifesto pledge — increasing rate from 14→18% on BADR isn't enough, full income-rate treatment was promised but softened).
- **Cost:** US ~$15bn/decade; UK ~£600m–£2bn/year.

### 10.8 Closing the "Step-Up in Basis" / "Uplift"

- **What:** End the rule that wipes out capital gains on death. Heirs should inherit the *original* cost basis, not the stepped-up market value.
- **Where:** US (Biden proposed it in 2021 — defeated by opposition from Manchin and the farm/real-estate lobby); UK (the Office of Tax Simplification recommended removing the CGT uplift at death — government ignored it).

### 10.9 Reforming the Non-Dom & "Golden Visa" Regimes

- **What:** Abolish non-dom regimes (UK did this in 2025 — major win), abolish or tighten "golden visa" / citizenship-by-investment schemes (Cyprus — after the Al Jazeera expose — Malta, Portugal's housing-pressure changes).

### 10.10 Unitary Taxation / Formulary Apportionment

- **What:** Instead of arm's-length transfer pricing (which is gaming paradise), tax a multinational as a *single entity*, apportioning its global profit by a formula (sales, payroll, assets — the way US states already apportion among themselves).
- **Where:** US states have done this for a century; the EU has repeatedly proposed "Common Consolidated Corporate Tax Base" (CCCTB) — blocked by Ireland, Luxembourg, Netherlands.
- **Why it matters:** Eliminates the profit-shifting problem at its root.

### 10.11 Public Country Registers of Trusts

- **What:** Trusts are the last bastion of Anglo-Saxon secrecy. Civil-law countries (esp. France, Germany) and the Tax Justice Network have demanded public trust registers for years. The EU's 5th AML Directive requires company registers to be public; trust registers remain restricted.

### 10.12 Global Asset Register

- **What:** A worldwide register of who owns what — building on the work of Gabriel Zucman and the Tax Justice Network. Would let tax authorities see cross-border wealth and tax it.
